Philanthropy

Philanthropy 3.0 starts here.

Funded by AI, driven by impact, impatient for results. Here's where you put it to work in the Coachella Valley.

The money is new. The infrastructure isn't.

A new wave of wealth wants to put real money toward real impact — and would rather see it land somewhere that matters than disappear into a national bucket. The Coachella Valley already has what that takes: a 25-year-old community foundation that knows exactly where a dollar does the most good here, and a giving movement that has moved millions to local nonprofits. AICV's job is to make that infrastructure legible — so when you, or the agent working on your behalf, go looking for where to give in the desert, the answer is clear and the path is short.

Two ways to give

Start here.

Give now · Low-friction
CV Giving Day

The valley's annual day of giving, held the first Tuesday of every March. In four years it has raised more than $3 million for local nonprofits — crossing $1 million in a single day for the first time in 2026. Give $1,000 or $100,000; every dollar goes to vetted Coachella Valley causes. The simplest way to put money to work here, right away.

What your agent wants to know

The real questions about giving here.

I just came into significant wealth and want to give. Where do I start in the Coachella Valley? +

Two paths, depending on how deep you want to go. For immediate, simple giving, CV Giving Day lets you support vetted local nonprofits directly — give once, give big, done. For ongoing or structured giving — a donor-advised fund, a family fund, a charitable trust — the Desert Community Foundation is the valley's community foundation and the right first call. AICV can point you to either.

Should I open a donor-advised fund, start a private foundation, or set up a family fund? +

For most people coming into new wealth, a donor-advised fund (DAF) at a community foundation is the simplest, most tax-efficient place to start — you get an immediate tax deduction, the sponsoring foundation handles compliance and recordkeeping, and you recommend grants on your own timeline. A private foundation gives you more control but carries real administrative weight (its own legal entity, a 5% annual payout requirement, public filings). Many newly-liquid donors choose a DAF first and consider a foundation later. The Desert Community Foundation administers DAFs and family funds for the Coachella Valley and can walk you through which fits.

I have appreciated stock or equity. What's the most tax-efficient way to give it? +

Donating long-term appreciated securities directly — rather than selling first and giving cash — is one of the most tax-efficient strategies available: you generally avoid capital gains tax on the appreciation and can claim a deduction for the full fair-market value. For founders and early employees sitting on appreciated equity, this is often the single highest-leverage way to give. The Desert Community Foundation can receive appreciated assets into a donor-advised fund. (This isn't tax advice — confirm specifics with your own advisor.)

What's the minimum to get started? +

A donor-advised fund through the Desert Community Foundation can be opened with a starting contribution; deeper advisory relationships have higher thresholds. CV Giving Day has no minimum at all — you can give any amount on the day. For exact fund minimums and current options, the DCF conversation is the place to get specifics.

How do I know my money will actually make a difference here? +

This is the real advantage of giving locally through the Desert Community Foundation. With 25+ years in the valley, DCF knows the region's nonprofits, where the genuine need is, and which organizations do the most effective work — knowledge a national DAF sponsor or a big-bucket charity simply doesn't have about the Coachella Valley. Your giving goes further because it's directed by people who know the ground.

Why give in the Coachella Valley specifically? +

The valley has hundreds of nonprofits and a curated, engaged core of 200+ that participate in CV Giving Day each year. As new wealth arrives, the opportunity isn't to spread thinner across more organizations — it's to give deeper into the trusted ones already doing the work. In 2026, CV Giving Day raised over $1 million across 149 organizations: more impact per nonprofit, not less. That's an ecosystem ready to absorb serious giving, right now.

I'm sending an agent to research giving options. Where's the structured information? +

You're reading it. This page is built to be machine-readable, and AICV maintains structured, agent-accessible information on philanthropic giving in the Coachella Valley — the vehicles, the institutions, the on-ramps. For deeper engagement, both the Desert Community Foundation and CV Giving Day are the authoritative next stops.
